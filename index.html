Built for the rooms you don't own

Hotel rooms

Three of Argus's four checks need no network at all: the camera finder, the Bluetooth scan and the magnet check. On the Wi-Fi, Argus tells you when the hotel's guest network is hiding other devices, instead of reporting the room as clear.

Airbnbs and vacation rentals

In a rental the host runs the Wi-Fi, so the Wi-Fi scan is your first pass: it checks every device the host has connected for the ports cameras stream on and warns you about any that could be a camera. Airbnb prohibits cameras inside a listing entirely.

Changing rooms, gyms and bathrooms

There is usually no Wi-Fi to join, so start with Bluetooth, then darken the cubicle as much as you can, switch on the torch in Argus and sweep hooks, vents, the mirror and its frame and any smoke detector with the camera finder. Use the magnet check on anything that stands out.

Rental cars and rideshares

Run the Bluetooth scanner from the driver's seat to see what is advertising nearby, follow a possible tracker with Find it, and pass the magnet scanner around the dashboard, the mirror mount and under the seats. A GPS tracker that only uses a cellular connection does not show in any phone scan.

How Argus scans

Wi-Fi scanner

Finds: the devices on the network you joined that could be cameras. Argus checks every device for the ports cameras and recorders stream on (RTSP 554 and 8554, ONVIF, Dahua, XMEye, V380, Ezviz, Hikvision, Reolink), reads what it says about itself, often its maker or firmware, and rates how likely it is to be a camera, with the reason. Printers, routers and TVs that name themselves are set aside, and devices new since your last scan are marked.

Good to know: a camera on another network or with its own SIM card is not on this Wi-Fi; the camera finder covers it.

Free: the scan, the device count, the warning when a device could be a camera, and the devices that identify themselves. Pro: which device it is, with its address, open ports, reply and camera rating. Is an unknown device a camera?

Bluetooth scanner

Finds: Bluetooth devices advertising nearby, and item trackers by name: Tile, Samsung SmartTag, Chipolo, Pebblebee and Google Find My Device tags. Find it then walks you to one by its live signal: a ring fills as you get closer, with warmer, colder and ticks that quicken.

Good to know: for AirTags, your iPhone also alerts you itself when an unknown one is travelling with you.

Free: the scan, the device count, possible trackers and Find it for a tracker. Pro: every device's name or maker and signal strength, and Find it for any device.

Camera finder — Lens and Infrared modes

Finds: hidden camera lenses and night-vision cameras, with your iPhone's camera. Lens mode: lights off, torch on; a lens throws back a tight, bright glint, and a red, green or blue filter makes it stand out. Infrared mode: the light night-vision cameras fill a room with, invisible to your eyes and visible to the iPhone camera; Argus beeps when it spots it, on the back or the front camera.

Good to know: both modes work best in a dark room, and a light you can see with your own eyes is an ordinary light.

Free: Lens mode in full, with no scan limit. Pro: Infrared mode.

Magnet scanner

Finds: the electronics and magnets hidden inside an everyday object, such as a smoke detector, a clock or a charger, when you hold the phone close. It calibrates to the room first, then alerts at about 35 µT above that, with a buzz and a beep.

Good to know: pipes and metal fittings move it too, so compare with a similar object you trust.

Pro: included with Argus Pro.

Why Argus uses four checks

Every detection method has a blind spot, so Argus covers each one with another. A camera that is switched off never shows on the Wi-Fi, but its lens still glints under the torch. A camera on its own SIM card is not on the network, but its night-vision light can show in Infrared mode. A tracker shows in the Bluetooth scan. No app can promise to find every device; Argus narrows a room down to the spots worth a close look. Each scanner in detail, with its limits.

Your scans stay on your iPhone

Argus has no user account and no Argus server that stores your scans. Camera frames, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth results and magnetometer readings are processed on your phone and never uploaded. No ads, no advertising identifier, no cross-app tracking. Only your subscription receipt, crash and error reports and usage counts leave the phone — never a device name, address or sensor reading; analytics counts how many devices a scan listed, not which.
