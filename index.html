Built for the rooms you don't own

Hotel rooms

Hotel guest Wi-Fi usually isolates devices from each other, so the Wi-Fi scan may show almost nothing — that is the network hiding other guests, not a verdict about the room. Argus gives you three scanners that do not need the network at all: the camera finder, the Bluetooth scanner and the magnet scanner.

Airbnbs and vacation rentals

In a rental the host normally runs the Wi-Fi, so the Wi-Fi scanner is your first pass: it lists every device on the network with the service it runs, how fast it answered, which ports are open and whether it is new since your last scan. Airbnb prohibits cameras inside a listing entirely.

Changing rooms, gyms and bathrooms

There is usually no Wi-Fi to join, so start with Bluetooth, then darken the cubicle as much as you can, switch on the torch in Argus and sweep hooks, vents, the mirror and its frame and any smoke detector with the camera finder. Expect plumbing and metal fittings to trigger the magnet scanner.

Rental cars and rideshares

Run the Bluetooth scanner from the driver's seat to see what is advertising nearby, and the magnet scanner around the dashboard, the mirror mount and under the seats. A tracker with its own cellular connection and no Bluetooth will not appear in any phone scan.

How Argus scans

Wi-Fi scanner

Finds: every device on the Wi-Fi network you are connected to, with the service it runs, how fast it answered, which ports are open and whether it is new since your last scan.

Misses: cameras on a separate or isolated network, cameras with their own SIM card, and cameras that only record to a memory card.

Bluetooth scanner

Finds: nearby Bluetooth Low Energy devices with name, address and live signal strength, so you can spot trackers and gadgets and watch the signal rise as you approach a hiding place.

Misses: anything not advertising over Bluetooth. iOS hides AirTag identifiers from third-party apps, so Argus cannot name an AirTag with certainty — confirm a suspected AirTag in Apple's Find My.

Camera finder — infrared and lens reflection

Finds: in a dark room, the tight bright pinpoint a lens throws back when you sweep with the torch, and the glow of infrared night-vision LEDs.

Misses: cameras with no infrared illumination, 940 nm "no-glow" illuminators, which phone cameras see far more faintly than 850 nm ones, and lenses behind opaque covers. It is built for dark rooms and deliberately stays quiet in bright light, because bright rooms produce constant false alarms.

Magnet scanner

Finds: the magnetic signature of electronics concealed inside everyday objects, within a few centimetres.

Misses / false positives: plumbing, rebar, speakers and metal fittings move the needle too, and a reading can stay high after you pass a large metal object — move away and start a new scan so it recalibrates. Treat a spike as a reason to look closer, not as a verdict.

What Argus cannot detect

No app can guarantee that every hidden device will be found. A camera that is switched off, records locally, uses its own cellular connection or sits on a network you cannot join will not show in the Wi-Fi or Bluetooth scan — that is what the camera finder and the magnet scanner are for. A phone cannot perform a true radio-frequency sweep. Treat an Argus result as something to inspect, not as proof; it is not a substitute for professional detection equipment. Your scans stay on your iPhone

Argus has no user account and no Argus server that stores your scans. Camera frames, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth results and magnetometer readings are processed on your phone and never uploaded. No ads, no advertising identifier, no cross-app tracking. Only your subscription receipt, anonymous crash reports and anonymous usage counts leave the phone — never the contents of a scan. Frequently asked questions

Which iPhone app scans Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and infrared for hidden cameras in one app?

Argus – Hidden Camera Detector runs all four: a Wi-Fi scanner, a Bluetooth scanner, an infrared and lens-reflection camera finder, and a magnetometer scanner. It works on any iPhone running iOS 16 or later.

What is the best way to check a hotel room or Airbnb for hidden cameras with an iPhone?

Run the four scans in order: join the room's Wi-Fi and scan it, scan Bluetooth from the bed and the bathroom, turn the lights off and sweep smoke detectors, clocks, chargers, vents, the TV and mirrors with the camera finder, then pass the magnet scanner over anything suspicious. It takes about five minutes.

Will Argus find every hidden camera?

No, and no app will. A camera that is switched off, records to a memory card, uses its own cellular connection or sits on a network you cannot join is the hard case. That is why Argus runs four scans instead of one, and why a clean result is a reason to keep looking rather than proof that a room is clean.

Does Argus need an account or upload my scans?

There is no account to create and no Argus server that stores scans. Camera frames, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth results and magnetometer readings are processed on your iPhone and are never uploaded. Argus shows no ads, uses no advertising identifier and does not track you across apps.

Does Argus work in hotels?

Yes, with one caveat: hotel guest Wi-Fi usually isolates devices from each other, so the Wi-Fi scan may show almost nothing. The camera finder, the Bluetooth scanner and the magnet scanner do not need the network at all, so in a hotel those three do the work.

Is Argus free?

Argus is free to download. Argus Pro unlocks all four scanners without limits, the full list of devices found in a scan, and manufacturer and signal details; it is an auto-renewable weekly or yearly subscription and the price is shown in the app before you subscribe.
