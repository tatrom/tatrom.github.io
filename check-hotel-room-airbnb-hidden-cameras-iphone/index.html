How to check a hotel room or Airbnb for hidden cameras with your iPhone — a five-minute sweep with Argus

Updated 15 October 2026

Argus – Hidden Camera Detector is an iPhone app that helps you check hotel rooms, Airbnbs and vacation rentals for hidden cameras and suspicious devices. This is the sweep we recommend the first time you walk into a room you don't own. It takes about five minutes and uses all four scanners.

Where cameras hide in hotel rooms and rentals

  • Smoke detectors and motion sensors above or facing the bed
  • Alarm clocks and clock radios
  • USB chargers and power adapters left plugged in
  • Air vents
  • TVs, set-top boxes and soundbars
  • Lamps and picture frames
  • Mirrors facing the bed or the shower
  • The router or modem itself
  • Anything with a small dark glass circle pointed at the bed or the bathroom

The five-minute sweep, step by step

  1. Step 1 — Join the room's Wi-Fi and run the Wi-Fi scanner

    Argus lists the devices on the network you joined that answer a scan, how fast each answered, and which of the known camera and DVR ports are open. An open port commonly used by RTSP can indicate a streaming service; it does not confirm that the device is a camera. When a port answers, Argus asks it what it is and shows the reply, which often names the firmware and identifies the software far better than the port number alone, and printers, routers and TVs that name themselves are set aside. Look for anything you cannot account for. From your second scan of the same network range onwards, Argus also marks addresses that are new. Many hotels' guest Wi-Fi isolates devices from each other; Argus tells you when it does, instead of calling the room clean, and you move on to the next three scanners. The scan, the device count, an alert when a device looks like a camera and the devices that identify themselves are free; which device the alert is about, its open ports and its camera-likelihood rating are part of Argus Pro. How to tell whether an unknown device is a camera.

  2. Step 2 — Run the Bluetooth scanner from the bed and again from the bathroom

    Argus lists the Bluetooth Low Energy devices advertising nearby during a ten-second scan and recognises item trackers such as Tile, Samsung SmartTag, Chipolo, Pebblebee and Google Find My Device tags. If it hears one, tap Find it and walk slowly: a ring fills as you get closer, it tells you warmer or colder, and its ticks quicken as you near it. Look in bags, coat pockets, under seats and in furniture. The scan, the device count and finding a possible tracker are free; names or makers, signal strengths and Find it for any device are part of Argus Pro.

  3. Step 3 — Lights off, camera finder on

    Close the curtains, turn off the lights and open the camera finder. In Lens mode, switch on the torch and sweep smoke detectors, clocks, chargers, vents, the TV and every mirror up close: a hidden lens throws back a tight, bright pinpoint that stays in place as you move the phone. Then switch to Infrared mode and sweep the room again: night-vision cameras light it with infrared your eyes can't see, and Argus beeps when the phone's camera shows a small bright dot. Lens mode is free in full; Infrared mode is part of Argus Pro. Both are built for dark rooms.

  4. Step 4 — Pass the magnet scanner over anything suspicious

    Sweep slowly, a few centimetres from the object. A spike means concealed electronics or a magnet — or plumbing, rebar and metal fittings — so treat it as a reason to look closer, not a verdict. If the reading stays high, move away from metal and start a new scan so the app recalibrates. The magnet scanner is part of Argus Pro.

  5. Step 5 — Cross-check and decide

    One scanner hit is a maybe; two scanners pointing at the same object is a pattern. Argus keeps your Wi-Fi and Bluetooth scans in a local history on your phone, so you can compare rooms on the same trip.

If you find something

Don't touch it, unplug it or take it apart. Photograph it in place, with the time and the location. Report it to the host or hotel management and to the booking platform — Airbnb prohibits security cameras and recording devices inside a listing entirely — and to local police if it points at a bed or a bathroom. Keep your photos and your messages with the host. If you feel unsafe, leave and ask for another room.

What this sweep cannot find

A camera that is switched off, records to a memory card, uses its own SIM card or sits on a network you cannot join will not appear in the Wi-Fi or Bluetooth scan. A lens behind an opaque cover, or a camera with no infrared illumination, may not show in the camera finder. Frequently asked questions

Does a hidden camera detector app work on hotel Wi-Fi?

Only partly. Hotel guest networks usually isolate each guest's devices, so the Wi-Fi scanner may see nothing at all. The camera finder, the Bluetooth scanner and the magnet scanner in Argus do not need the network, so in a hotel those three do the work.

Do I need an account to use Argus?

No. There is no account to create and no Argus server. Camera frames and magnetometer readings are analysed on the phone as they arrive and are never recorded or saved; Wi-Fi and Bluetooth scan results are kept in the app’s local storage on your iPhone, where you can delete them, and are never uploaded.

Which iPhone do I need?

Any iPhone running iOS 16 or later.

How long does the sweep take?

About five minutes for a hotel room, and a little longer for a whole apartment.

Is Argus free?

Yes. Argus – Hidden Camera Detector is free to download, and its core checks are free with no scan limit, no account and no ads: the camera finder's Lens mode, which spots hidden camera lenses in a dark room; the Wi-Fi scan, which warns you when a device on the network could be a hidden camera; and the Bluetooth scan, which counts possible trackers and walks you to one with Find it. There is no free trial that turns into a charge. Argus Pro, an optional weekly or yearly subscription, shows which device it is and adds Infrared mode for night-vision cameras and the magnet check.

Do I need a subscription to check a hotel room or Airbnb?

No. The core checks are free, with no scan limit and no trial that turns into a charge: the camera finder's Lens mode, the Wi-Fi scan with its camera warning, and the Bluetooth scan with finding a possible tracker. Argus Pro is for when you want to see which device it is, with its camera rating, open ports and details, and it adds Infrared mode and the magnet check. For a stay of up to a week the weekly plan is enough: you can turn off auto-renewal in Settings → your name → Subscriptions right after subscribing, and Pro stays active until the end of the week.
